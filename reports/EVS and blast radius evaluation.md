# Measure consequence, not exploits, to rescue the evaluation

The evaluation is not failing because the EVS or the blast radius is weak. It fails because the labels cannot tell any two scorers apart, and because one part of it checks a quantity the score never uses. Part A rests on **one exploited pair out of 9 labelled pairs (0 of them randomly sampled)**. That pair is a KEV-listed, CVSS 9.8, EPSS 0.99995 Jenkins CVE that every scorer ranks first, so every ladder rung and baseline ties at NDCG@10 = 1.000. A3 has **0 positives among 1,707 items**, and A2 is empty on the 17-host rerun. Part B shows strong agreement for Algorithm 1 (per-pair F1 0.973), but it validates BR_1, while the EVS number uses a separate unbounded, outbound-only P(u) that moves the score by **at most +6%** in this lab. Part B's truth sets were also written from the same design intent as the graph edges. The fix keeps the professor's structure (ladder L0→L4 with baselines, variants B0–B5) and changes what each rung asks:

- tie-aware and few-positive metrics;
- held-out-signal variants that break the KEV/SSVC circularity;
- graded, score-stratified, Horvitz–Thompson-weighted pentest labels;
- a blinded expert panel for construct validity;
- a 16-subset Shapley ablation;
- metamorphic tests;
- blast-radius truth taken from real scenario pivots and an independent emulation tool.

Almost all of this lives in `eval_kg/` and `evaluation/`. The BR_1-versus-P(u) mismatch, the 10.0 ceiling and the missing Evaluation section in the paper are method decisions for you, not script fixes. Realistically, a 17-host lab can support strong claims about correctness, about how much context reorders priorities, and about expert agreement. It cannot support a statistically significant claim that EVS predicts exploitation better than EPSS or KEV, and the write-up should say so up front.

## One exploited pair decides every Part A number

The headline Part A table is uninformative by construction. In the 17-host run, L0–L4, EPSS, KEV, SSVC and WazuhVD all score NDCG@5 = NDCG@10 = MRR = 1.000, and every difference is +0.000 [+0.000, +0.000] ([eval_score_ablation.md](../data/reports/eval_score_ablation.md)). The cause is label supply. `pentest_results.csv` holds **11 rows, 9 yes/no, 1 yes** (Jenkins CVE-2024-23897), and none of them came from the sampler ([pentest_results.csv](../evaluation/pentest_results.csv)). The sampled candidates are all still `not_tried`, and **17 of the 20 are local library CVEs** (`service=False`). Many will never be triable within a one-hour time box ([pentest_candidates.csv](../evaluation/pentest_candidates.csv)).

The forward-KEV test A3, which the plan called "the biggest gain", has **0 positive CVEs at T0 = 2026-05-01 and at most 2 at any T0 from 2023 to 2026**. The whole inventory holds only 8 KEV CVEs ([eval_kev_forward.md](../data/reports/eval_kev_forward.md); [evaluation.md](../docs/evaluation.md)). A2 had 5 scenarios and 6 instances on the 8-host run but is empty on the 17-host run ([eval_score_ablation.md, committed](../docs/reports/eval_score_ablation.md)). That looks like a regression in alert-window matching, not a lack of data, because S08–S13 exist.

Three metric artifacts sit on top of the scarcity:

- **Tie-breaking by item id.** `metrics.ranked` breaks ties by item id ([metrics.py](../eval_kg/metrics.py)). At L0 the positive is tied at 9.8 with two Web negatives, and "Jenkins|…" sorts first alphabetically, so L0's 1.000 is an artifact. If those three items are the only tie, the expected NDCG over random tie orders is about **0.71** and the expected MRR about 0.61. L4 separates the positive (10.0 against 9.3) and keeps 1.000. Honest tie handling would therefore *reveal* the one small real L0→L4 gain that the current tie-break hides. The same artifact produced the A2 "L0–L4 = 1.000", which the project's own docs concede is "the id tiebreak" ([evaluation.md](../docs/evaluation.md)).
- **Conditioned bootstrap.** `paired_bootstrap` skips resamples where NDCG is undefined. With one positive, roughly 37% of item resamples contain no positive, so the reported CIs are conditioned on the positive being drawn and collapse to [0, 0] ([metrics.py](../eval_kg/metrics.py)).
- **Saturated metrics.** NDCG@10 on 9 items is just full-list NDCG, and P@10 is capped at 0.100 by the single positive.

Circularity runs through the score side as well:

- E = H whenever a CVE is in KEV or SSVC is "active" ([cvss31.py](../score_kg/cvss31.py)). KEV and SSVC are also standalone baselines, and the only positive is a KEV CVE, so L3/L4 cannot be separated from the KEV baseline on these labels.
- The "SSVC" baseline computes mission prevalence from the asset's CR/IR/AR (`ladder.py`). It is therefore partly environmental and not a context-free comparator.
- In A2, `technique_matches=True` turns ATT&CK-inferred CVEs into instances and marks them `alerted: True`, which sets E = H for them. The scoring CLI deliberately avoids this ([run.py](../eval_kg/run.py); [evaluation.md](../docs/evaluation.md)).

The deepest problem is construct validity. In a container lab, whether a CVE is exploitable depends mostly on whether the vulnerable feature is configured and reachable. No EVS input models that. CR/IR/AR, zone-based MAV and P(u) describe *how bad it is if exploited*, so rungs L1 and L4 have no causal reason to predict a yes/no exploit label, even with many more labels. The label taxonomy adds noise. The README files "feature could not be reached" under `not_tried` ([evaluation/README.md](../evaluation/README.md)), but several recorded `no` rows are "not configured" outcomes (S09). Some rows also lack timestamped evidence ([pentest_results.csv](../evaluation/pentest_results.csv)). This is the label-inaccuracy and sampling-bias pattern that Arp et al. found widespread in top-venue security ML papers ([Arp et al., USENIX Security 2022](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity22/presentation/arp)).

## Part B validates a set the score never uses

Part B looks good on paper: per (asset, CVE) pair, B4 reaches P/R/F1 0.974/0.999/0.973, against 0.825/1.000/0.873 for B5 (k=2) and 0.581/0.999/0.642 for undirected B3 ([eval_blast_radius.md](../data/reports/eval_blast_radius.md)). Four problems undercut it.

**First, it tests the wrong object.** `blast_radius` is Algorithm 1: a k = 1 BFS, reversed for disruption. `propagation`, which feeds the EVS, calls `distances(edges, asset)` with no depth bound and no reversal ([score_kg/model.py](../score_kg/model.py)). The abstract says the "security requirements of the nodes within the affected zone are used to calculate the EVS", but in the code BR_1 never touches the number. P(u) is per asset and ignores the impact mode m(c), so it cannot reorder CVEs on the same host. On the 17-host lab, P(u) peaks at 0.1211 (Jump, ×1.0605 uplift), and **8 of 17 assets have P = 0**. The 1/(|V_A|−1) normaliser also makes P shrink as the lab grows: Nginx fell from 0.125 on 8 hosts to 0.0547 on 17 ([scoring.md](../docs/scoring.md); audit computation on [lab/asset_edges.csv](../lab/asset_edges.csv)). `distances_for_p` gives B4 and B5 the same P, so the "downstream" row is identical by construction and flat at 1.000 on A1's single positive ([variants.py](../eval_kg/variants.py)). A reviewer can say "Part B validates a set you don't use in the score", and they would be right.

**Second, the truth is not independent of the model.** `lab/asset_edges.csv` says its edges are "the intended application flows, each exercised by the source host's health check in lab_env/eval/checks.csv". The dependents truth set comes from those same checks, and the usable truth set comes from finding credentials the team itself planted ([asset_edges.csv](../lab/asset_edges.csv); [measure.py](../eval_kg/measure.py)). The measurement is live and mechanically separate, but predictor and truth share one author's design intent. F1 near 0.97 is therefore close to tautological.

**Third, the metrics hand out free credit.** `set_prf` gives an empty prediction precision 1 and an empty truth recall 1, and averages are macro over hosts ([metrics.py](../eval_kg/metrics.py)). Because 8 of 17 hosts have no usable truth and 7 have no dependents, **B0, which has no blast radius at all, scores F1 0.471 and 0.412** ([eval_blast_radius.md](../data/reports/eval_blast_radius.md)).

**Fourth, the design is never stressed where it matters.** The zone predicate π prunes none of the 12 CONNECTS_TO edges, so nothing tests the zone constraint of Eqs. 5–6, and B3 against B4 tests only direction. On the network truth set, B4's recall is 0.088 and zone-level B2 is perfect. The docs explain this away after the fact ("the network set alone would favour B2") ([evaluation.md](../docs/evaluation.md)). Using usable/dependents as primary is defensible, but only if that choice is written down before rerunning.

The literature context helps here. Most attack-graph papers offer scalability results plus one case study, not predictive accuracy. Poolsappasit et al.'s TDSC evaluation used an **8-host, two-subnet test network** ([Poolsappasit, Dewri & Ray, IEEE TDSC 2012](https://cs.du.edu/~rdewri/data/MyPapers/Journals/2011TDSC.pdf)). The rare realistic test, MulVAL against two attacker teams on 199 machines, found that hosts predicted *impossible* to compromise were more than 3× more likely to be compromised, and the authors blamed scanner input quality ([Sommestad & Sandström 2015, abstract only; full text not read](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1108%2FICS-06-2014-0036)). Measuring real compromise and dependency sets in a small lab is therefore *above* the field's usual bar. The problem is independence, not size. No source the research found evaluates a directional (compromise vs. disruption) blast radius against measured outages, so the "dependents" truth set is a defensible novelty.

## A revised design keeps the ladder and variants but changes their questions

The design below keeps L0→L4 and B0–B5 visible as the professor asked. It adds rungs ordered by evidential strength, and every rung states which labels or signals it shares with the others. Effort is in person-days; "yield" is what the 17-host lab can realistically deliver.

### Statistics plumbing comes first because it changes today's numbers

Before any new label exists, `eval_kg/metrics.py` and `eval_kg/run.py` need four fixes:

- **Tie-aware metrics.** Compute the expected value over random orderings within tied groups, either exactly or by Monte Carlo, instead of breaking ties by id.
- **Few-positive primary metrics.** Use AP, the rank of each positive, AUC as "fraction of negatives below the positive", and **effort-to-coverage / work saved over random**. The EPSS line of work made efficiency, coverage and effort the vocabulary vulnerability-management reviewers recognise ([Jacobs et al., EPSS v3, arXiv 2302.14172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14172)). AP is preferred over ROC under imbalance ([Saito & Rehmsmeier 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0118432)).
- **Stratified paired bootstrap and exact tests.** Resample positives and negatives separately so every resample is defined, and add a paired permutation test and exact McNemar on top-k discordant positives. McNemar is the test with acceptable Type I error for single-run comparisons ([Dietterich 1998](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~tgd/publications/dietterich-approximate-statistical-tests-nc1998.pdf)). Randomization tests are preferred over Wilcoxon and sign tests in IR ([Smucker et al., CIKM 2007](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/getpdf.php?id=744)).
- **Holm correction within pre-declared families.** Use three families: ladder increments, L4 against baselines, and Part B variants against B4 ([statsmodels multipletests](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.multitest.multipletests.html); Holm 1979 cited from the standard reference, unverified in the notes).

Add a **random baseline**, a **CVSS×EPSS** baseline and a **context-free SSVC** (fixed mission prevalence) next to the current SSVC. Add an EPSS-style rule table: in KEV, Internet-reachable, CR=H, public exploit exists. This takes about 1–1.5 days. It immediately changes the 17-host A1 table from "everything = 1.000" to roughly L0 ≈ 0.71 against L4 = 1.000. That is honest but n = 1, and it should be reported as an illustration, not a result.

## Four decisions belong to you, not to the evaluation scripts

The audit turned up several issues that no change to `eval_kg/` can fix honestly. These should go to you and your professor as explicit choices, not be patched quietly.

**D1: BR_k does not feed the EVS.** The abstract implies that the affected zone's security requirements drive the score. In the code, BR_1 (Algorithm 1) produces a reported set, and P(u) uses an unbounded outbound reachability sum ([model.py](../score_kg/model.py)). There are three options. You can make P(u) sum over BR_k(a,c), which changes the code and Eq. 16 and makes the score depend on the CVE's impact mode. You can re-word the paper so that BR_k is the reported affected-asset set and P(u) is a separate network-position term, which changes text only. Or you can keep both and let B10 show the gap. Running the B10 numbers first would make this an evidence-based choice.

**D2: P(u) is direction-blind, normalised by network size, and nearly inert.** Because it ignores m(c), it cannot reorder CVEs on the same host. The 1/(|V_A|−1) normaliser means adding unrelated hosts dilutes every P. With β = 0.5 the term adds at most 6%. Any change to β, γ or the normaliser is a method change. Tuning it on these labels would be test-set snooping, so the evaluation should only sweep it (A5).

**D3: the min(10, ·) ceiling.** Saturation at 10 erases discrimination exactly where triage matters. In this lab every alertable CVE starts at 9.8. Removing the cap, or reporting the uncapped value as a tie-breaker, is a paper and code decision. The evaluation can only report how many items saturate.

**D4: lab changes outside the eval scripts.** Three would help, and each sits outside the eval scripts:

- one CONNECTS_TO edge that crosses a denied zone pair, so π is exercised on real infrastructure;
- confirmed CR/IR/AR for the 9 hosts whose values are still "drafted from the role, not yet confirmed" ([lab/assets.csv](../lab/assets.csv));
- an outside person who plants or audits credentials for the usable truth set.

All of them touch `lab/` or `lab_env/`.

Two smaller items fall inside the eval scope but change what a rung means, so you should confirm them. The first is dropping alert-based E = H for technique-inferred A2 instances. The second is replacing, rather than adding to, the CR/IR/AR-informed SSVC baseline.

Separately, the paper has **no Evaluation section at all**: its headings stop at "III. Our Proposed System". So "consistency with the paper" constrains the method only, and the evaluation section is yours to write. ## What a 17-host lab can honestly claim

The evaluation can support five claims:

- the implementation matches its specification (A0, with mutation score);
- context materially reorders priorities: τ-b 0.682 against CVSS, with a count of CVSS ties broken (A6);
- EVS reproduces blinded expert prioritisation better than CVSS, *if* A4 shows it, labelled as construct validity;
- Algorithm 1's directional k = 1 set matches measured usable and dependency impact better than undirected, zone-level, deeper and centrality alternatives, with the independence caveat stated, and corroborated or contradicted by real pivots and an independent emulation tool;
- the sampled pentest results are *consistent with* or *inconclusive about* L4's advantage, with HT-weighted CIs and a stated minimum detectable effect.

It cannot support three claims:

- that EVS predicts exploitation better than EPSS or KEV, given at most a handful of positives and E built partly from KEV;
- that P(u) improves rankings: its expected Shapley share is near zero, and it is not what Part B validates;
- that any of this generalises beyond one lab with one team's asset valuations.

The rungs are not independent. A1, A2 and the expert panel share items, and E shares signals with the KEV and SSVC baselines. Any statement that "several rungs point the same way" must name these overlaps. A Fisher or Stouffer combination is only valid across rungs that share no items or labels.

Presentation can make a small lab convincing without overclaiming. The precedents are a TDSC paper whose whole evaluation was an 8-host network with more than 20 scenarios ([Poolsappasit et al.](https://cs.du.edu/~rdewri/data/MyPapers/Journals/2011TDSC.pdf)), and graph prioritizers such as VulRG, whose evaluation consists of re-run case studies and "rankings move when topology changes" with no outcome labels ([Jiang et al., arXiv 2502.11143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11143)). Measured truth plus sampled pentest labels already clears that bar. Five elements will do the persuading:

- an **evidence-ladder table** that names each rung, its truth source, the signals it shares, and its result with CI;
- one **end-to-end walk-through figure** for a single alert (graph → BR → P(u) → EVS) and a per-scenario table (foothold, predicted BR, achieved set, P/R);
- a **leakage matrix** mapping each label to the inputs it could leak into;
- the **EPSS-style effort/coverage table** with single-rule strategies;
- a threats-to-validity section that cites the Sommestad negative result as the reason this kind of validation matters.

Inconclusive A1 results should be reported as "directionally favourable, ceiling-limited, minimum detectable effect = X", never as "no effect" ([Card et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.06595)).

Several notes flag their sources as unverified:

- the Sandström thesis (search snippet only);
- Kaynar 2016, Holm 1979, Efron & Tibshirani, Bradley–Terry/`choix`, Lundberg & Lee, Carterette's minimal test collections and the Manski bounds (all cited from memory);
- Krippendorff's α thresholds and the Hanley–McNeil constants;
- Bozorgi 2010, Allodi & Massacci 2014, TESSERACT and Järvelin & Kekäläinen (marked citation-known);
- CAVP, the 2026 Computers & Security article and the LLM-triage preprint (snippets or a 403);
- SCyTAG's CALDERA integration.

Check these before they enter the paper.

## Conclusion

The current evaluation asks a question the lab cannot answer, namely whether context predicts *exploit success*, and it ignores the one the paper actually poses: whether context changes *priority given consequence*. Moving Part A's labels toward graded consequence and blinded expert judgement, and moving Part B's truth toward attacker-produced and third-party-measured reach, keeps the professor's ladder and variants intact while making each rung able to fail. Being able to fail is what makes a success on that rung believable.

The most important finding for the paper is not statistical. The blast radius the evaluation celebrates does not enter the score, and the term that does enter it is nearly inert. Resolving that (D1/D2) before the next rerun will decide whether the paper's final claim is "graph context reprioritises vulnerabilities" or the narrower, still defensible "asset requirements and exposure reprioritise vulnerabilities, and a directional one-hop blast radius correctly identifies the affected assets."
